What this prototype is

SIH 26067 — INCOIS Ocean Valley

Two data sources, switched in the header. The coastline and sea floor are real in both.

REAL MODE · CYCLONE AMPHAN, MAY 2020

  • Model: HYCOM GOFS 3.1 global analysis (US Navy, 1/12°), eight analyses at 12 UTC, three days apart, 4–25 May 2020. Averaged into the app's 0.5° cells and 11 levels (75 m and 750 m interpolated between model levels). Temperature, salinity and currents.
  • Argo: real profiles from the Argo GDAC, with delayed-mode or adjusted values where they exist and only QC flags 1, 2, 5 and 8 kept; for each snapshot, each float's profile closest in time (within 1.5 days). The same density-inversion check runs on them.
  • Cyclone: Amphan's six-hourly positions and winds from IBTrACS v04r01 (JTWC and IMD). The yellow ring marks where the storm was at the snapshot shown.
  • Coast and sea floor: ETOPO1, sampled every 0.1°.
  • Read with care: HYCOM assimilates Argo, so the model–Argo comparison is a consistency check, not independent validation. HYCOM has no chlorophyll, and there is no public glider data in this window.

The snapshot is built by data-pipeline/build_snapshot.py (Python, numpy only) and embedded in this file, so real mode also works offline.

SYNTHETIC MODE

The 3D field, Argo floats, and glider tracks are procedurally generated, shaped to match real Arabian Sea / Bay of Bengal oceanography (surface warming, a shoaling thermocline, Bay-of-Bengal freshening from river runoff, monsoon-driven gyres).

The observed profiles are synthetic too: each instrument samples a "true" ocean that differs from the model the way real ones do — a displaced thermocline, a warmer or cooler mixed layer, a fresher river-plume layer in the Bay that models under-resolve, sensor noise, and missing levels. The model is interpolated to each observation's position and depth, and the misfit is reported as bias and RMSE in the spirit of GODAE OceanView Class 4 verification. Profiles are checked for density inversions (> 0.03 kg/m³); flagged levels are left out of the statistics and drawn as red rings. One float carries a deliberate bad salinity sample to show this.

The drifting current streaks move at a visually tuned speed, not a physically scaled one — they're there to make circulation direction and structure legible at a glance. Named-current labels mark the general region where each real current is known to occur.

The whole app, including Three.js r128 and the real snapshot, is inside this one HTML file, so it runs with no network connection. Only the web fonts are fetched online; offline it falls back to system fonts.

WHAT PRODUCTION WIRING NEEDS

  • A backend service (xarray / PyNIO) parsing NetCDF over OPeNDAP, exposing a REST/WMS-WCS layer so the frontend never touches raw files.
  • Argo/glider ingestion normalizing IFREMER NetCDF profiles into the same lat/lon/depth/time/variable schema used here, on a scheduled pull.
  • CF-Conventions-compliant metadata so new variables (chlorophyll, ADCP currents, HF-radar, moorings) register as new layers with no frontend re-engineering.

OPERATIONAL VALUE (WHY THIS MATTERS TO INCOIS)

  • Cyclone intensity forecasting — tropical cyclone heat potential (TCHP) is the heat stored above the 26 °C isotherm (D26). This view computes it live from the temperature and salinity columns and maps it, with the D26 surface underneath. The 20 °C isotherm (D20) is the usual proxy for thermocline depth.
  • Search & rescue — surface current vectors let forecasters estimate drift trajectories instead of reading them off static charts.
  • Fishery advisories — chlorophyll profiles from Argo/BGC floats mark productive zones without a dedicated desktop tool.
  • Public science communication — the same 3D view that helps a forecaster also makes ocean dynamics legible to students and policymakers.
